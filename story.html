San Felipe Creek - Background behind this Story

Hi, I'm Joel Owens. Ordinary guy.

I think a lot of people remember the highlights of their younger years, and they remember them fondly.

Over time, we tend to wash out the bad and leave only the good. Maybe we just exaggerate the good, and that's where we come up with sayings like "The good ol' days".

Probably some wisdom could be learned from Carly Simon's "Anticipation", where she reminds us that these are the good old days. Often, we don't recognize them while we are living them.

It's around the age of Junior High that I and others I have visited with experienced what they felt in their hearts was their first love. And those are powerful emotions at an age where emotions carry a lot of weight. Of course, very few of those first-loves ever actually pan out for either person. But the memories are hard to shake, and as time moves us forward the highlights of those days are remembered more and more with happy thoughts and events that made them feel so special. Sometimes those memories can bring back a lot of hurt and even some damage to our sense of self-confidence that followed us like a cloud for a long time. As a result, the interactions we have during those times, especially those of a close friendship and maybe more than that, played a larger part of forming us than we like to acknowledge.

As for me, some of the less happy of those memories left me comparing myself in a negative way to other people around me, and I was doing it with a measuring stick that wasn't fair. I learned over time that most of us are average people. We don't belong on magazine covers or on TV shows. But attractiveness, popularity and social standing aren't a measure of a person's worth. But there is goodness and grace and kindness in a lot of people.

In my book, I wanted to show ordinary people, living with ordinary struggles, and some struggles that were much harder than others, but from those things come people who care deeply, do kind things without external forces to push them there, who think of the needs of others as more important than their own (Phillipians 2:1-4). Those people are amazing, and they bring with them the life-energy that spreads to others who are around them.

I wanted to remind myself and you, my friend, that sometimes we don't get what we pray for. In fact, some prayers feel like they are answered 'No'. We feel like God either didn't hear us, or he knew something we didn't, or even worse we consider that God may have ignored our needs. Because of that, we might think He continues to ignore us as we struggle to keep our heads above the waters of sadness and grief. As Michael would say in this book "It's still important to pray." We know that often we pray for others, and then death, unforeseen, enters instead of the healing and recovery we hoped would come. I have a very distinct memory of praying with a family whose father and husband had just been loaded onto an ambulance in front of their house. I saw the ambulance and because I knew them, I stopped. He was conscious, talking, and I remember praying as though I somehow knew he was going to be just fine. A couple of days later, I was asked to officiate his funeral. I did my best to put together a message of hope and love, but believe me - I stood at that pulpit with the sensation that the rug of my prayer life had been pulled from under me. Understanding was a long distance beyond my horizon. In my book, I referred to an old hymn called "Farther Along" which states that things may happen and we may lose loved ones, our houses will feel empty and lonely, and we won't be able to understand why in this lifetime. But in the scope of eternity, not only will understanding come, but joy will come with it. I keep the small flyer from his funeral inside my Bible as a reminder that God has His timing, and that I should never get too comfortable with the notion that the gift of another day is just around the corner. It might not be there. (2 Peter 3:10-12)

So, about this story. You might be asking me if I believe these things to be true and the stats not really in anyone's favor, why did I write a happy reunion into this book? I want you to know that I struggled with the events of this book, because many pieces of it are my own story to tell. I won't tell you which, but just for you to understand the way I came to cope with this is to make peace with the people of my past as best I could, honor those who lifted me, or who pointed the way from their own lives, and to create an ending where patience, honesty, doing the right things no matter what the cost could rest peacefully with me.

People touch our lives in different ways. They say one thing that encourages us, or they give us a sheet of paper with a hand-drawn schematic that fascinates us at a young age, or they give us train-whistle codes, or that one piece of sage advice that we somehow manage never to forget. I wanted to honor that. To lift it high as an example of what we should be to those who are young and looking for direction. I wanted to honor the feeling of love at a young age and to remember how powerfully it forms us and makes us dream of what we wish for in life. It teaches us how to treat others with respect and dignity, and to see the very best in those we are priviledged to live close to.

San Felipe Creek was quite literally one alley-way from behind the house where I lived in Del Rio during my Junior High years. I spent many hours there, in all seaons, swimming and wading in the heat of those days, standing in my coat skimming rocks along the surface, sitting on the rock wall high over the channel below me, dreaming and thinking, planning and hoping for wonderful things in life. It was a magical place to me, and a magical time to be there. I had many good friends from school and band, several of whom I keep in touch with even today. My model USS Enterprise hung from the ceiling in my room, spinning slowly with the breeze that occasioned through the windows. Many of those things did not survive the move from there, and losing them only added to my sensation of being in a strange world where I knew nobody, where there was no creek or any nearby water. The adjustment caused me to come out of my shell and to give real effort to my life, to make those new friends, to find places I could enjoy, and to even spark those feelings once again that I never thought I would find. Well, believe me, I found them. And I lost out a few times, as did we all. But in time, things followed God's plan and fell into place they way He planned for them to.

So, my book may have a 'fairy-tale' ending. But I think it's the summary of what we can all feel today, if we wil just look with appreciation and love for the people God has placed us with and the many gifts and blessings we have around us.

Give love.

Joel
